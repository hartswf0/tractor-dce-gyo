Abstract

The WAG Studio Suite materializes a philosophy of visible infrastructure. Built on LDraw—where LEGO bricks exist as lines of code—the suite treats the context window as the scarcest resource in computing. Its architecture—canonical MPD files, skeleton ownership graphs, broadcast-bus synchronization—reflects the shift from "prompt engineering" to "context engineering" (Karpathy, 2025). By preserving thick description (Geertz), captured grammar (Agre), and seamful design (Chalmers), the suite offers a model for human-AI collaboration grounded in the thirty-year history of the contested digital brick.

Ontology Types (C-T-A-M-D-S-P-I) ↑ top

The Parallel Genealogies: The WAG Studio Suite and the LDraw ecosystem share a common grammar—a way of thinking about creative work as traceable, accountable, and regenerable. To demonstrate the ontology types defined below, we use James Jessiman and the thirty-year history of the digital LEGO brick as our running example.

Every type in the WAG Genome finds its prototype in the LDraw story.

These types form a genome schema for tracking creative provenance—who made what, when, why, and how. This structure draws from David Spivak's Ontology Logs (ologs) for knowledge representation.

C
Creator
The human author whose decisions shape the trail—the accountable actor.
"The point is that agency, once constituted by the intersection of material and social phenomena, can be called to account for itself, and is thus inextricably linked to power." — Suchman, L. A. (1987). Plans and Situated Actions, p. 182

LDraw Example: James Jessiman's situated action was to take the Community side in a dialectical struggle—not through rhetoric, but through code. When he died in 1997, his agency could be "called to account" because it was legible. The .dat files he authored carried his name in the !AUTHOR header. The community could continue his work because his situated actions were visible in the Trail.

T
Trail
The evolving history of a creative work. A temporal sequence of artifacts and decisions.
"The owner of the memex... runs a trail of his interest through the maze of materials available to him... He adds marginal notes and comments... He builds at will an associative index which works in accordance with the truly marvelous manner of the human mind." — Bush, V. (1945). As We May Think. The Atlantic

LDraw Example: The LDraw library is not a static database—it is a Trail, an evolving sedimentary record. The library is governed by SteerCo and LSB; new parts undergo peer review. 3001.dat (the 2×4 brick) was authored by Jessiman in 1995; later parts carry the names of community members. Because the file format was plain text, it was inspectable, editable, and immortal—a "White Box" ecosystem.

A
Artifact
Concrete outputs: MPD files, JSON manifests, GLB exports. The "objectified" creative moment.
"A cognitive artifact is a material object whose physical structure has been shaped by its participation in cultural practices... they function in the distribution and coordination of cognitive functions." — Hutchins, E. (1995). Cognition in the Wild, p. 354

LDraw Example: Jessiman's most elegant artifact was not a brick—it was stud.dat, a single file representing the knob on top of a brick. Every other part references this file; if you improve stud.dat, you improve thousands of parts instantly. The architecture is recursive: 3001.dat does not contain geometry—it contains calls to primitives. One file update upgrades the entire history of digital LEGO.

M
Metadata
Timestamps, file-learnings, birth dates. "Data about data"—the invisible infrastructure.
"Infrastructure is defined as an embedded, standardized, transparent, and ultimately invisible set of resources that supports a wide range of social and technical activities." — Bowker, G. C., & Star, S. L. (1999). Sorting Things Out, p. 34

LDraw Example: Every LDraw part file begins with metadata: !AUTHOR traces provenance, !LICENSE enables redistribution, UPDATE signals certification. When this metadata works, it is transparent—users don't think about it. When it breaks, coordination fails. Metadata is LEGO's social contract encoded in text.

D
Decision
Architectural choices: "use stud_skeleton," "adopt Frank bus." Traces of reflection-in-action.
"The practitioner allows himself to experience confusion and uncertainty... he frames the situation, and conducts an experiment in place. He is engaged in a reflection-in-action which disappears when it is successful." — Schön, D. A. (1983). The Reflective Practitioner, p. 68

LDraw Example: Jessiman defined a custom coordinate unit: 1 LDU = 0.4 mm, with studs being 12 LDU diameter. This integer-based system avoided floating-point errors on early 90s hardware. The decision "disappears when successful"—users today don't know they're using LDU. But the coherence of the entire 10,000-part library depends on this single architectural choice.

S
Tool
Software that transforms artifacts. Swiss, COURAGE, GOLD, MENTO. The instruments of creation.
"An instrument is an artefact dedicated to the accomplishment of an action... The fundamental idea is to turn abstract operations into manipulable objects." — Beaudouin-Lafon, M. (2000). Instrumental Interaction. CHI '00

LDraw Example: Jessiman didn't just define a file format—he wrote LDraw.EXE, the first viewer. The open format enabled a Tool ecosystem: LDView (1998), MLCad (2000), LPub (2002), BrickLink Studio (2018). The "White Box" architecture of LDraw meant that anyone with Notepad could participate in the ecosystem—unlike the "Black Box" of LEGO Digital Designer.

P
Pattern
Recurring structure detected across artifacts. The reusable "core of the solution."
"Each pattern describes a problem which occurs over and over again in our environment, and then describes the core of the solution to that problem, in such a way that you can use this solution a million times over." — Alexander, C., et al. (1977). A Pattern Language, p. x

LDraw Example: Line Type 1 (The Reference) allows a single stud.dat file to be referenced thousands of times without duplicating geometry. This is Alexander's pattern made literal: never hardcode geometry; always reference a primitive. The syntax 1 <color> x y z ... stud.dat embodies what Parnas (1972) called "information hiding."

I
Intent
The creator's purpose. The "why" behind artifacts—the thick description.
"What... transforms a twitch into a wink... is the structure of significance... which the anthropologist describes as 'thick description'." — Geertz, C. (1973). The Interpretation of Cultures, p. 7

LDraw Example: The thin description: to render LEGO bricks. The thick description: while Darwin burned through budget in a corporate lab, an Australian teenager wrote a program to model his own creations. The Intent was grounded in the hacker ethos of the mid-90s: a teenager who couldn't afford every brick could now model them all. This was liberation from scarcity—preservation of the "Platonic Ideal" of the brick.

Morphisms (Relationships) ↑ top

Following category theory (cf. Spivak's Category Theory for the Sciences), these are structure-preserving maps between types. Each morphism has a prototype in the Jessiman story.

generates : C → T
A Creator generates a Trail through sustained practice.
LDraw: James Jessiman generated the LDraw Trail from 1995 to 1997—a library of foundational parts, a file format specification, and the first viewer. After his death, the community continued the Trail. Today, the LDraw library contains over 10,000 "certified" parts.
contains : T → {A, M, D}
A Trail contains Artifacts, Metadata, and Decisions.
LDraw: The LDraw Trail contains Artifacts (10,000+ .dat part files), Metadata (!AUTHOR, !LICENSE headers), and Decisions (LDU coordinates, Type 0-5 line syntax, the primitive reference pattern). The Trail is sedimentary—each layer depends on the layers beneath it.
transforms : S × A → A'
A Tool transforms an Artifact into a new Artifact.
LDraw: LDraw.EXE transformed .dat files (text) into rendered images (pixels). LPub transforms MPD files into building instructions (PDF). BrickLink Studio (2018) transforms LDraw files into high-fidelity renders. Each transformation preserves structure.
syncs : Frank × {S₁, S₂, ...} → coherence
Frank bus synchronizes multiple Tools into coherent state.
LDraw (Analog): In the pre-internet era, LDraw coherence was maintained through mailing lists. LUGNET forums served as the "Frank bus"—broadcasting part updates, certification decisions, and bug reports. What was social infrastructure in LDraw is technical infrastructure in WAG.
derives : A → stud_skeleton
An MPD Artifact derives a stud_skeleton ownership map.
LDraw: Every LDraw model implicitly contains an ownership structure: which brick owns which stud. Jessiman's design enabled this derivation—because bricks were defined in a coordinate system (LDU), ownership could be computed geometrically. The skeleton was latent in the format from the beginning.
supports : S → I
A Tool supports the Creator's Intent.
LDraw: Jessiman's Intent was liberation: freeing the brick from scarcity. His tools supported this by making the brick inspectable (plain text), editable (any user could modify coordinates), and immortal (text survives where binary formats decay).

Conclusion: The Jessiman Morphism

The LDraw history is not just an example—it is a prototype. Every type in the WAG Genome finds its first instantiation in the Jessiman story:

Creator (C)James Jessiman
Trail (T)LDraw.org library
Artifact (A).dat part files, stud.dat
Metadata (M)!AUTHOR, !LICENSE headers
Decision (D)LDU coordinates, primitive pattern
Tool (S)LDraw.EXE, LDView, MLCad
Pattern (P)Primitive reference, modular decomposition
Intent (I)Liberation from scarcity

The morphism Jessiman → WAG is structure-preserving. What was true for the contested digital brick remains true for the contested AI context window.

Jessiman saw that a brick could be a word. We see that a context window can be a scene. The gesture is the same.

📄 The DCE-GYO Labs Toolkit: A Reflective Account

1. Toolkit Description

The DCE-GYO Labs Toolkit (WERE, WEAVER, MASTER) comprises a set of second-order tools—tools designed for understanding and repairing the core architecture of the primary WAG suite. The core function is to ensure structural coherence between the scene's geometry (the MPD file) and its underlying ownership graph (stud_skeleton.json).

The toolkit exemplifies anti-bundler, single-file HTML architecture, often embedded together in a shell like TIMBER. Users interact via direct manipulation and visual feedback (WERE's colored overlays). Input: MPD file + existing stud-skeleton data. Output: repaired stud_skeleton.json (WEAVER) or validation report (MASTER).

2. Issues Addressed

The Labs address a critical Reflection Gap and Visibility Gap inherent to complex, generative systems with loosely coupled, message-bus (Frank Bus) architecture:

  • Reflection Gap: Design work proceeds too fast for structural consistency checks, leaving the "space of illegal states" representable in memory.
  • Visibility Gap: The stud-skeleton—mapping geometric parts to ownership—is invisible. When errors occur, the system's breakdown is opaque.

The Labs resolve this by making the invisible ownership graph visible (WERE), rebuildable (WEAVER), and formally checkable (MASTER).

3. Audiences

  • Primary: Researcher-practitioners, system architects, and expert builders engaged in major editing or integration tasks.
  • Secondary: All end-users of DCE-GYO tools (COURAGE, GOLD)—the Labs ensure core stability.
  • Tertiary: Future developers and maintainers who use the tools as documentation-in-action.

4. Context of Use

The toolkit is designed for the Breakdown Phase of the design cycle—explicitly invoked when the primary tool's transparent operation fails (when ready-to-hand becomes present-at-hand). Primary contexts:

  • Audit: MASTER before export
  • Debug: WERE/WEAVER after major, high-risk edits

Workflow: After a major merge or global transformation, run WERE to inspect → WEAVER to rebuild → MASTER to validate the Hoare Postcondition before marking the artifact safe for downstream use.

5. Levers and Leverage Points

Following Shilton's (2013) values levers framework:

  • Integration: The stud-skeleton embeds explicit Ownership (analogous to Rust's linear types) into the technical specification.
  • Reflection: WERE forces reflection by making the invisible breakdown visible via Seamful Design (Chalmers & Galani, 2004).

Applying Meadows' (1999) leverage points hierarchy:

  • Level 9 (Information Flows): WERE/WEAVER make the critical stud-skeleton generation transparent and modifiable.
  • Level 6 (Rules): MASTER introduces a Hoare Postcondition—preventing illegal states from entering the export pipeline.

In Lessig's (2006) regulatory modes, the system relies on Architecture: the Labs form structural constraints that elevate good practice to required practice.

6. Design Process

The Labs emerged through a reactive, autoethnographic design process driven by failures of the loosely-coupled system. The core insight came from LDraw's history—treating the brick as a "linguistic construct" enabled "textual inspectability."

WERE was born from the frustration of selection failing to sync in COURAGE. This revealed that the "Billion Dollar Mistake" (null references/orphan studs) was the system's greatest vulnerability. WEAVER was then built to actively repair this by constructing a rigid skeleton, inspired by the desire to implement Hoare Logic visually.

7. Learnings and Reflections

Building the Labs Toolkit revealed that rigidity is the precondition for agency in a generative system. Key insights:

  • "Parse, Don't Validate" is a design philosophy: MASTER taught that merely flagging an error is insufficient; the architecture must make the illegal state unrepresentable or un-exportable.
  • Breakdown is a Feature: By making the present-at-hand moment productive, the Labs turn failure into a mechanism for learning and repair.
  • The true locus of integrity: Not the MPD file alone, but the stud_skeleton—the rigid ownership graph.

If starting over: Prioritize making the stud-skeleton a primary, version-controlled artifact from day one, rather than allowing WEAVER to be an afterthought for repair.

8. Future Directions

  • Integration: Connect MASTER output directly to the GOLD editor's undo stack, allowing "step back" to the last valid state.
  • Generalization: Abstract the ownership model and Hoare Logic validation into a library for other generative systems—implementing "Rust for X" ownership in any domain.
  • Validation: Conduct user studies comparing recovery rates in seamless COURAGE vs. seam-exposed TIMBER environments.

The Linguistic Brick

On LEGO, LDraw, and the Architecture of Context

An LDraw file is plain text. It is hierarchical. It is human-readable. It constrains infinite geometric possibility into a coherent scene. The MPD format—Multi-Part Document—is a scene graph encoded as ASCII, where submodels reference submodels in recursive descent.

In the era of Generative AI, a context window is the same thing.

Researchers call the entropy that accumulates when unstructured tokens flood a model's memory "Context Rot." The antidote is structure. The antidote is hierarchy. The antidote is the captured grammar of a well-designed prompt.

In our research, we posit that the MPD file is the context window. The stud is the token. The library is the retrieval corpus. And the governance problem—who certifies which parts enter the canon—is the "Alignment Problem," translated into geometry.

This is the LEGO-AI Entanglement. To understand it, we must look back thirty years to a moment where the "digital brick" split into two distinct ontologies.

Darwin vs. LDraw: The Black Box and the Open Text

In 1995, the LEGO Group was burning through millions developing "Darwin"—a secretive physics simulation running on Silicon Graphics supercomputers. Darwin attempted to simulate "clutch power"—the satisfying resistance when two bricks snap together. It treated the digital brick as a physical simulation, locked inside a massive binary file called db.lif—uneditable, unextendable, and proprietary.

Simultaneously, an Australian teenager named James Jessiman wrote the code that would outlast them all. He called it LDraw.

Jessiman defined the LEGO brick not as a mesh, not as physics, and not as an asset locked in a corporate vault, but as language.

Darwin (The Image)
Binary, sealed, heavy.
LDraw (The Word)
Plain ASCII, open, inspectable.

Jessiman didn't care about physics; he cared about syntax. His .dat files were scripts. A 2×4 brick was a reference to a library file (3001.dat), positioned in integers because early '90s hardware couldn't handle floating-point math.

This distinction is pure Operative Ekphrasis. Darwin tried to represent the brick through simulation (Ekphrastic Hope). LDraw performed the brick through text (Operative Ekphrasis). Darwin ran on machines that cost more than houses and is now dead. LDraw ran on anything, and files from 1996 still open today.

The Syntax of the Stud

The "Linguistic Brick" is built on a modular architecture that prefigures modern AI tokenization. Consider the stud.

In the physical world, a stud is a cylinder. In Jessiman's world, the stud was stud.dat: a single file defining that geometry. Every other part in the library referenced this file. Thousands of parts, all pointing at the same few lines of code.

This is Void Management. If you improved stud.dat—perhaps adding triangles for smoother renders—you improved every part that used it, instantly. This hierarchical efficiency allowed for infinite scaling on limited hardware.

But a language needs a dictionary, and a dictionary needs editors. When Jessiman died in 1997 at the age of twenty-five, a "martyrdom effect" solidified his hobbyist project into a sacred text. The LDraw.org Steering Committee (SteerCo) and Standards Board (LSB) emerged to govern the library. Part authoring became democratic yet strictly peer-reviewed. New parts had to be "certified" before entering the official collection.

This was the first successful attempt at solving the Alignment Problem for digital matter. The community built a "White Box" where the grammar of the brick was transparent, unlike the "Black Box" of LEGO's official tools, where users waited helplessly for corporate patches.

The Antidote to Context Rot

In our project, we utilize this history to solve the problems of current Generative AI. When we prompt a video generator with natural language ("Make a LEGO castle"), the model hallucinates. It suffers from context rot; the geometry melts.

By using LDraw as the input—by using the Linguistic Brick—we provide the model with a rigid skeletal structure. We feed the AI the "captured grammar" of the MPD file.

In 2018, the ecosystem finally converged when BrickLink acquired LEGO's rendering technology. The Corporation and the Community merged. But the cultural victory had been won decades earlier.

It was won by a young man in Australia who saw that a brick could be a word.

He proved that text is durable. That syntax survives where physics perishes. And that words, unlike plastics, do not wear out.

Philosophy of Science & AI Patterns

Patterns grounded in new materialism, quantum philosophy, and LLM research.

situated-action
THEORY
Plans are resources for action, not specifications for it. The actual course of creative work is always situated, improvised, and emerges from immediate context.
"The idea that a person's behavior is directed by a plan suggests that the actual course of action... is governed by the plan... Our analysis points to the opposite conclusion: plans are resources for situated action, not specifications for it." — Suchman, L. A. (1987). Plans and Situated Actions, p. 59
Interpretation: This pattern grounds the flexibility of WAG tool use. The Core Tools define a "Golden Path" (a plan), but the ultimate creative action is always situated, improvised, and emerges from the user's interaction with the immediate data environment.
intra-action
PHILOSOPHY
Entities don't pre-exist their relations—they emerge through entanglement. Creator and Artifact are mutually constituted in the act of creation.
"The notion of intra-action recognizes that the 'differentiating' of the 'components' of phenomena... is enacted from within the phenomenon and does not precede it." — Barad, K. (2007). Meeting the Universe Halfway, p. 139
Interpretation: The WAG Suite is a phenomenon where knower and known (Creator and Artifact) emerge together. Knowledge (e.g., a complete MPD model) is intra-actively produced at the moment the Creator's Intent and the Tool's capabilities entangle, rather than being a property of either component alone.
agential-cut
PHILOSOPHY
The performative boundary that resolves ontological indeterminacy. Design decisions that separate "subject" from "object" where no prior distinction exists.
"The agential cut is a performative resolution of the ontological indeterminacy... it enacts a separation between 'subject' and 'object' (in contrast to the more familiar Cartesian cut which takes this distinction for granted)." — Barad, K. (2007). Meeting the Universe Halfway, p. 140
Interpretation: The agential-cut is the design boundary (e.g., the 1-million token window, the JSON schema format) that the system imposes on the probabilistic "Void." This cut is a performative act that resolves the inherent ambiguity of the AI's latent space, making a specific reality (the constrained completion) accessible to the user.

worlding
PHILOSOPHY AI
Creating self-sustaining simulated ecosystems with their own laws, metabolism, and "bag of beliefs." The imposition of a deterministic grid upon the wilderness of generative possibility.
"A world is not a narrative but a gated garden with laws and borders... it is the imposition of a grid upon the wilderness of generative possibility." — Cheng, I. (2018). Emissaries Guide to Worlding (paraphrased)
Interpretation: Worlding defines the architecture of the AI environment. The WAG Suite itself is a "gated garden" (the Frank bus ecosystem) where Void-Management imposes deterministic laws (LDraw geometry, stud-skeleton coherence) upon the open-ended possibility space, shifting the user's role from consumer to Caretaker/Architect.

context-rot
AI
The empirical degradation of LLM performance when relevant information is placed in the middle of long contexts. The "U-shaped retrieval curve."
"Language models are better at retrieving information from the beginning and end of the context... performance drops rapidly when relevant information is placed in the middle of the input context." — Liu, N. F., et al. (2024). Lost in the Middle. TACL, 12, p. 158
Interpretation: The empirical finding of the "U-shaped retrieval curve" grounds context-rot. The pattern acknowledges that model performance degrades as irrelevant data accumulates, necessitating active Void-Management strategies to place critical information at the "edges" of the context window to prevent entropy.
Design Response
Void-Management: actively curate context, prune stale information, anchor critical data at beginning/end
void-management
AI THEORY
Active curation of the AI context window to counteract context-rot. Treating context as finite RAM that must be managed, not a bottomless log.
Academic Grounding

Context as RAM: Karpathy (2024) "Software 3.0" — context window is the new execution environment.

Lost in the Middle: Liu et al. (2024) — empirical evidence for why management is necessary.

Strategies

Prune stale/irrelevant history
Use structured formats (JSON, MPD) as "jars"
Periodic summarization checkpoints
