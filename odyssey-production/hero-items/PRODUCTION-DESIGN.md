## What was compared

The recovered Halfworld atlas contains **102 props, set pieces and vehicles**. `artifact-inventory.json` retains their actual states, parameters, anchors and transitive scene imports. An import is evidence of availability, not proof that an object appears on screen. “No matched native build” means no matched build in this recovered pipeline; it does not assert that an asset never existed elsewhere.

The supplied review movie demonstrates strong LEGO imagery. Its three short excerpts do not establish the individual state changes, grips and functional mechanisms below. The previous 55-second Bed Test established timing and recognition blocking; its bed was still a simple frame with a rooted-post suggestion.

## Three new tests

Each is an 18-second, 1280×720, 12 fps **silent mechanical/blocking study**, not a completed adaptation of the source scene. Original scene durations, voice tracks and narrative beats remain in the existing intake contracts.

1. **Bed:** made → bedding lifted away → root cutaway → attempted movement. The rooted assembly never translates.
2. **Stake:** heat → four-person carry → advance → roll against an eye-height target. One stake; four independently measured grip contacts. The final cave and giant are explicitly absent.
3. **Axes:** lane establishment → gauge traverse → close proof view. Stock bow is a rejected continuity reference. This does not claim a working bow release.

## Repeatable production loop

1. **Read the actual artifact and scene together.** Extract state, ownership, contact anchors, source timing and required camera evidence. Normalized atlas anchors are not world coordinates.
2. **Find the failure that would invalidate the shot.** Bed moves; stake splits into spears; arrow collides; rope detaches; hand loses object; mechanism becomes invisible.
3. **Forage canonical LDraw geometry.** Record part ID, actual dimensions, native scale, catalogue image and rejection reason. Inspect holes and molded sub-elements, not just names.
4. **Build the smallest connected mechanism.** No nonuniform resizing of rigid parts. Keep the whole prop under one named transform; use separate articulated groups only where the mechanism needs them.
5. **Block actors around contacts.** Grip locations, foot support, collision corridor, ownership and handoff come before the final camera. Do not alter the source meaning to fit a convenient accessory.
6. **Render adversarial views.** Actor-height wide, contact close-up, reverse angle, clearance-axis view, and a root/mechanism cutaway if needed. Preserve the failed pass.
7. **Measure, repair, repeat.** Bounded geometry checks, fixed-root transforms, number of independent objects, contact error, palette and silhouette. View the frames; numeric tests cannot approve composition.
8. **Promote only after the entire beat works.** Then add source voice, complete entrance/action/exit timing and edit context. A handsome catalogue render is not a scene-ready asset.

## What this loop actually repaired

- First bed roots were occluded. Reverse cutaway exposes the trunk-to-root continuity; actual stump and printed cap replace generic block roots.
- First floor/leg levels were inconsistent. Floor and feet were brought to the same datum; only the root quadrant cuts away.
- First stake bearers overlapped in projection. Staggered longitudinal contacts and a higher three-quarter camera make the four-person mechanism clearer.
- A direct legacy LDraw `Raycaster` call stalled. A finite triangle traversal now audits geometry; the original failed approach is not treated as a successful check.
- Attractive axe candidate rejected after its socket failed the through-bore test. The new lane passes eight radial probes.

## Boundaries

These are digital staging assemblies, not certified physical LEGO instructions. Brick connection legality and clutch strength are not yet tested. Stake locomotion, two-handed grips, full Cyclops performance, functional bow release, final arrow and film-ready decoration remain open. No paid image/video model, external publishing or PR changes were used for this pass.
