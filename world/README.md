# The world, in one page

`word-to-world.html` is one page and one loop. Everything below lives in `world/*.js` and is wired in `world/main.js`.

## The loop and the modes

`tick()` measures the frame and runs `simulate(dt)` in fixed substeps (up to four of 1/60 s; a slower frame slows the world rather than jumping it), also from the test hook `__world.step`. `W.stats` carries `ms`, `fps`, `steps`; when the frame rate stays low the **quality** tier drops (pixel ratio, city radius, crowd cap) and comes back when it recovers; `?quality=auto|high|low` and the menu row pin it (`world.quality`). The player is in one **mode**:

| mode | who moves | camera | input |
|---|---|---|---|
| `walk` | the minifig rig (`minifig.js`) | over the shoulder (`Minifig.camera`) | left thumb walks, right looks; tap right swings or shoots; two fingers Force push |
| `fly` | the TIE (`tie.js`) | chase (`Tie.camera`) | drag carves, second finger boosts, tap fires, hold torpedoes |
| `ride` | a vehicle prop (`drive.js`) | chase (`Drive.camera`) | left thumb drives or flies, second finger boosts, tap fires, hold fires heavy |
| build (a flag on `walk`) | the rig | reticle aim | palette, tap places |

## The sky

Night is published as `S.night` and fanned out by `nightFall` in main.js: the city's lit panes (`paneMat` emissive), the lamps, a ride's headlights, the TIE's engine glow and the page's vignette (`--vig`). The Death Star is `litAlways`.

The stars sit on the dome with depth testing on, so the ship and the buildings occlude them. Each world's `lights` preset colours its lamps and windows: Hoth cold and sparse under a bright moon, Tatooine amber with two suns by day, Endor torches on the footways with fireflies at night, the Death Star white. `Ground.daylight` makes the two lights and the fog once; `Worlds.apply` paints a preset's day colours and palette; `W.sky.set` (in `setWorld`, `setSky`, `setWeather`) then blends them by the sun's height and the weather and moves the sun light. `auto` reads the device clock at the place's latitude and longitude; `day / dawn / dusk / night` pin the sun. Weather is `clear / cloudy / fog / rain / storm` (a storm has lightning). Both persist (`world.sky`, `world.weather`) and take `?sky=` / `?weather=`. The page's chrome follows the horizon colour (`--sky`, `--skyA`, `body.dark`). Minifig accessories: a hand's grip is 9.9 LDU ahead of its origin at 14.5° (3820.dat); `Minifig.toolMount` puts a tool there, bars turned to rise from the fist, and the aim pose levels the blaster.

## Words to bricks

```
words ──ai.js──▶ program {name, ops} ──dsl.compile──▶ pieces (cells → tiler → support) + props
       stream  ▲                        │                       │
       op by op│                        ▼                       ▼
        partialProgram          dsl.toRows (LDU)        Props.place (MPD)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                             Build.addRows → InstancedMesh
```

- `ai.js`: the brain. One text per turn to the Responses API, streamed. `ask` = (plan →) design → local check → review (skipped when the design was slow or needed a lower effort); `edit` and `repair` are one call. `requestSafely` retries at lower reasoning when the model runs out of output room. `onStatus` and `onDelta` feed the page. **What the model gets**: the DSL spec and the master-builder standard as the system prompt; as the user message the place, the planet, the ground, the nearest five things by kind, name, distance and bearing, the free room in studs in each direction from the origin (`mbContext`), the brief, and a picture of the view (`snapshot`: one render into a 640x360 JPEG, sent as `input_image`, the `look` button turns it off). A big brief (seven words or more, or two things named: `needsPlan`) gets a **plan** first (`Ai.plan`: `{name, scale, parts: [{name, role, at, size, col}]}`), whose parts stand as ghost boxes at the anchor (`mbPlan`) while the design is asked to build one `group` per part; `Dsl.checkPlan` then says which part vanished or missed its box, and the reviewer gets those notes, the picture of the view and a picture of the first design as built (`snapshotDraft`). An edit that names a group (`Ai.groupIn`) asks for that group alone and the log says which groups changed and which were kept. **The genes** (`Ai.genes()`: the rules, the demos, the plan threshold, the router, the selector's weights) come from `world/manifest.json` (`Ai.loadManifest`, defaults in code until it loads); `Ai.manifest()` hashes the genes and the kernel (the spec and the compiler) and every receipt and log line names the hash; `Ai.route(report)` is the repair router; `quality()` reads the selector gene. The kernel (the DSL, the compiler, the evaluators) is code and never a gene.
- `receipts.js`: one record per build asked of the model, kept in IndexedDB (`world.receipts`, the newest 500): the manifest, the brief, the context (the picture's size, not the picture), the plan and its notes, the raw answer, the program, the compile report, the residual (attempted against committed: unknown, errors, floating, blocked, vanished), the cost (calls, tokens, reasoning, seconds, pictures), a 320x180 picture of the draft, and the after-actions with their seconds (`committed`, `changed` with the words and the group, `nudged`, `discarded`, `saved`). `receipts` in the menu copies them as JSON for the foundry.
- `dsl.js`: the build language (`SPEC`), the compiler, `decompile` (bricks → program), `caption` (program → words), `partialProgram` (a half-written answer → its finished ops). Every piece carries the op that made it. A `group` op (`{op:'group', name, x, z, y, turn, size, ops}`) is a named subassembly: its ops are written in the group's frame (`placeIn` shifts and turns them, rect ops as rects, cell ops as cells, facings with them), every piece carries the group's index (the code panel folds it, a tap lights the whole part), `report.groups` lists them with what they laid and `report.vanished` the ones that laid nothing; `flatOps` opens groups for words and counts; `checkPlan(plan, res)` compares the planned parts with the groups built.
- `main.js` builder section: `mbDraft` (words → draft), `mbEdit` (words about a draft), `readBuild` (what stands → program), `mbCommit` (draft → bricks, or one rideable prop when `drive`/`fly` is chosen), the code panel (`paintOps`, `litOp`), the log (`mbLog`), the library (`saveBuild`).

### The foundry (`tools/foundry/`)

The harness as an experiment: the kernel is frozen, the genes are a manifest, the evidence is receipts. `node tools/foundry/replay.js receipts.json` compiles every receipt's program again under the current kernel (drift) and classes the failures (empty, broken, residual, discarded fast, reworked, ran out of room). `canaries.js [--manifest m.json] [--receipts r.json]` runs the cases in `cases/` (failed builds with their expected class and route, and pairs the selector must rank: a gate over a plain box, a shop over the same shop with a floating roof, the bridge over a bigger slab of bricks) and fails a manifest that regresses one. `demos.js receipts.json --out candidate.json` picks demonstrations by evidence (committed as they came, nothing lost in the compile, one per subject) into a candidate manifest. `compare.js A.json B.json [--live]` applies the promotion contract: the same kernel, one gene changed, canaries under both, then paired trials over `briefs.txt` within a token ceiling (`--live` with `OPENAI_API_KEY`), and answers NOT_YET with the receipts still missing (repeated seeds, blind recognition, human preference, situated tests) or REFUSED; a manifest is promoted by a person committing `world/manifest.json`. `metric.js` judges one program from stdin (class, residual, quality, route, the plan check) for the DSPy track, `dspy_builder.py` (needs `pip install dspy`), which optimises only the instruction and the demos against that metric and writes a manifest, never code.

## Cinerium: the performance

`cinerium.html` is the film page with a performance panel; the engine is two modules the page loads before the film. **`cinerium.js`** is the register: `Perform.CHANNELS` names the toy's one-axis joints with their clamps (root yaw, hips pitch, torso lean, roll and twist, head yaw, each arm's pitch and outward angle, each hand's roll, each leg's pitch, the hips' drop) and the face's features (brows up, knit and asym, eyes wide, narrow and blink, gaze, jaw, width, press, smile, frown, asym, cheek, teeth, tongue). Every figure actor gets a performance (`Perform.attach`): a base, `PHRASES` blended in and out (the Odyssey's twenty directions as channel values, `headPitch` landing on the torso's lean since a head cannot pitch, and the body phrases the halfworld scores name: arms crossed, open arms, hands near face, shrug, swagger, panic, deadpan, sit), keyed `tracks` on the reel clock, `speech`, `life` (small residual motion during holds, seeded per actor) and blinks. `Perform.sample(P, t)` gives the vector; `Perform.apply` writes it into the pivots after the walk and the pose, on a **stepped clock** (`quantize`: on twos, twelve holds a second, unless the shot carries `SPEED` below one, when it runs smooth: the stop-motion hack). **Projection**: `lookAt` puts a bearing into head yaw up to 80 degrees, the excess into the torso's twist and then the root, the height into the torso's lean; `reach` is one arm's pitch; nothing bends an elbow. **Actions** (`PERFORM "who" TURN "target" AT s LEAD eyes s FOLLOW head s FOLLOW torso s`, `LOOK` with `KEEP gaze`, `GLANCE`, `LEAN`, `REACH`/`POINT` L|R, `RAISE`/`LOWER`, `CROSS`, `HOP`, `RECOIL`, `SHAKE`, `NOD`, `HOLD "phrase" FOR s LIFE v`) resolve their targets when they fire and write keys with lead and follow. The grammar: `BEAT "id" "who" FROM s TO s WHY "..." DIRECT word` (the reason travels with the film), `SET "who" channel v AT s [OVER s] [CURVE ease|linear|hold]`, `PHRASE "who" name AT s [ENTER s] [HOLD s] [RELEASE s]`, `SPEAK "who" "text" AT s [FILE f FROM s FOR s]`, `FACE "who" name AT s` (a replacement head), `STEP "who" ONES|TWOS|THREES`, `LIFE "who" v`, `ASSERT "who" READS_AS word AT s [NOT word]` (a close-up of the actor kept with the word it should read as, `W.filmCloseup`), and the repair verbs `INCREASE`/`REDUCE "who" channel BY v`, `EARLIER`/`LATER`, `KEEP`; all of them events of the shot they stand under, round-tripped by `F.parse` and `F.text`. **`face.js`** draws a face on a plain head: a curved plane over the front of 3626b with a canvas texture, redrawn only when the vector changes (brows as strokes, eyes that flatten at the bottom when narrowed and at the top when wide, pupils from gaze, a mouth curve corner to corner with the jaw as its opening, teeth and tongue as fills, asymmetry a channel); and the speech engine ported from the halfworld: ten visemes, `visemeTrack(text, seconds)`, `sampleMouth` with a 110 ms triangular blend gated by the voice's energy, `speechCarriage` (small lagged head motion from the same envelope), `envelopeOf` (RMS at 50 Hz). A `SPEAK` plays the line (a synthesized `.ogg` or a slice of a recorded take by `FILE FROM FOR`, `Sound.loadFile`) and drives the mouth from its envelope (`<file>.env.json` beside a take, `env` in `lines/index.json` for a synthesized line), so live and offline read the same one. The panel (`cinerium-ui.js`): the beats with their reasons, a slider per channel of the selected actor (a slider writes a key; a Take records them as SET lines kept in the shot), the clock's setting, a close-up kept as a record, the sheet of assertions.

Two pipelines feed it, one scene each. **Reference → cuts → cameras → cast → scene** (`tools/cuts.js <video>`: ffmpeg scene detection into a cut list, a contact sheet and a MENTO skeleton of one SHOT per cut; `films/simpsons-intro.cuts.json`): `Film.SCENES['simpsons-intro']` (`world/scenes-cinerium.js`) is the intro shot for shot, 83 s in thirteen shots, with the Springfield cast as real LDraw sculpts (`Minifig.DEFS` `homer, marge, bart, lisa, maggie, flanders`: `parts` lists their own parts; a part the harvest does not carry loads through the shared loader with its print, `loadPrinted`; each head has two expressions on disk, swapped by `FACE`), bodies on twos, a `board` vehicle kind (a skateboard the rider stands on), builds only where a camera needs them, the `springfield` cue. **Halfworld → cinerium** (`tools/odyssey.js OD-B23-S04 --halfworld <clone>`): the scene's cast with its stage marks, the score's rows (poses as PHRASE, walks as acts, lines as SPEAK slicing the recorded voice by the manifest's segments), the direction table's beat as BEAT with its why and an ASSERT, the per-line directions as notes, a megaron BUILD, and the camera chosen per beat (a two-shot for the room, a close-up at each speaker): `Film.SCENES['odyssey-od-b23-s04']` (the bed test: recognition) and `['odyssey-od-b01-s03']` (the threshold: tenderness) in `world/scenes-odyssey.js`, the voice and its envelope under `world/lines/odyssey/`. The Ithaca cast (`penelope, odysseus, eurycleia, telemachus, phemius, athena, suitor`) wears the drawn `odyssey` face.

## Words to film

`word-to-momento.html` is the world page with `window.__momento` set and `film.js` loaded: main.js binds the film (`bindFilm`), routes words to `W.film.words` while the bar's switch says film, lets the film's camera stand where the shot says after the mode's own camera has run, walks the figure for an act instead of the stick (`W.filmGround` sets a ride or the TIE down for an act that needs the ground), and counts the steps of a take.
