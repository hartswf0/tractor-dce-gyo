# Chapter 39
## When THE TORTOISE meets THE DUCK:
## The Machine of Relations

**Michel Serres**

In a meadow, where the grass is long and the sky is high, a small, awkward creature crawls. He is **THE TORTOISE** (representing The Passenger/The Parasite). He carries his house on his back and dreams of flight.

Two birds land beside him. They are **THE DUCKS** (representing The Energy/The Engines).

‘I am tired of the back roads’, complains The Tortoise. ‘I want to see the country.’

‘We can help’, say The Ducks. ‘But you must hold on.’

They find a stick. The Ducks take the ends in their beaks. The Tortoise bites the middle. They lift off.

‘Look!’ cry the people below. ‘A miracle! A flying tortoise!’

The Tortoise, filled with pride, opens his mouth to speak. ‘I am the King of the Air!’

He falls. *Splat*.

‘You fool’, whispers the wind. ‘The machine works only if the third keeps quiet. You profit from the system, but you must not interrupt the channel. To speak is to fall.’

In a nearby swamp, a Frog ties his leg to a Rat’s leg.

‘Come to dinner’, says The Frog. ‘We will swim.’

But the Rat cannot swim. He struggles. A Hawk sees the commotion. He swoops down and grabs the Rat. Because they are tied, he gets the Frog too.

‘A machine’, observes the Hawk, ‘is a relation. A yoke. A beam. The third always wins.’

A man watches from a hill. He sees a Horse chasing a Deer. The Horse cannot catch the Deer.

‘I hate him’, says The Horse. ‘Help me catch him.’

‘I will help’, says the Man. ‘But you must let me saddle you.’

The Man gets on the Horse. They catch the Deer. But the Man does not get off. He rides the Horse to the stable.

‘The third position is the position of mastery’, says the Man. ‘I parasitized your hatred to enslave you. You thought you were hunting; you were being domesticated.’

He pats the Horse’s neck.

‘This is the origin of technology’, the Man explains. ‘It is not copying nature. It is copying our relations. The lever is a relation. The scale is a relation. The tool is the crystallized parasite. It intervenes. It mediates. It controls.’

He looks at his tools: a hammer, a whip, a computer.

‘Every machine is a strategy of enclosure’, he concludes. ‘Every technology is a way of turning a subject into an object. We objectify our relations to master them. We build the middle to control the ends.’

# ASSESSMENT 55: CANDIDATE "THE NAIVE VIEW"
**Theoretical Submission:** Technology as Extension of the Body / Instrumentalism

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate defines technology as a neutral extension of physical capabilities (Hammer = Fist, Wheel = Foot).
*   **Darkness Prior:** High risk of **Banalization**. As Serres notes, this view is "vaguely obscene and naive." It fails to explain complex machines or the social relations embedded in tools.
*   **Intended Frame:** Anthropological/Functional.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: The Origin of the Machine
*   **Evidence:** "Does the machine copy the body?"
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Empirical Failure**. A computer does not copy a brain; a lever does not copy an arm. They copy *relations*. The Naive View cannot account for the "well-run machine" which is a system of mediation, not just amplification.
    *   *Verdict:* **Shadow**.

#### Criterion B: The Neutrality of the Tool
*   **Evidence:** "Technology is a means to an end."
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Political Blindness**. It ignores the fact that the tool (the saddle, the yoke) transforms the user into a subordinate. The Horse is not extended by the saddle; he is enslaved by it.
    *   *Verdict:* **Void**.

### 3. Overall Grade: THE NAIVE VIEW
**Score: 40/100 (Obsolete)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The failure to see the "Third." Technology is never just User and Object; it is User, Object, and the **Mediator** (the Parasite).

# ASSESSMENT 56: CANDIDATE "THE MAN" (Serres)
**Theoretical Submission:** Technology as Objectified Social Relations / The Excluded Third

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate defines technology as the crystallization of parasitic relations. The machine mediates between two entities (Horse/Deer, Frog/Rat) to control them.
*   **Darkness Prior:** Moderate risk of **Universalization**. Is *every* relation parasitic? (Serres implies yes, structurally).
*   **Intended Frame:** Cybernetic/Sociological.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: The Triad (The Third Position)
*   **Evidence:** "A third exists before the second." "The Hawk holds the middle."
*   **Guardian Critique:** **High Analytical Power**. This reframes technology from a bilateral (Man-Nature) to a trilateral (Man-Machine-Nature) system. The Machine is the Interrupter that becomes the Master.
    *   *Strength:* Explains domestication as a parasitic strategy.
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion B: The Silence of the Machine
*   **Evidence:** "The talking machine is mortal." "The parasite must keep quiet."
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Deep Insight**. A functioning machine (or parasite) must be invisible to work. If the Tortoise talks, he falls. If the noise overwhelms the signal, the system crashes. Efficiency = Silence.
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion C: Objectification of Relations
*   **Evidence:** "Growing objectification of our intersubjective relations."
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Structural Integrity**. Tools are solidified social strategies. The saddle is the solidified relation of "Mastery." The computer is the solidified relation of "Logic."
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

### 3. Overall Grade: THE MAN
**Score: 99/100 (Distinction)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Paradigm Shift:** Serres moves the origin of technology from "Needs" (I need to eat) to "Relations" (I need to master the horse to catch the deer).
*   **Final Verdict:** The Man wins because he understands that **to build a machine is to build a trap**.
