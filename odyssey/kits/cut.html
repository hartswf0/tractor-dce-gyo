The Regulars' Cut

"This movie is long, can't bore the regulars." The halfworld film runs as long as its 152 voice recordings laid end to end. This is a cut of it made only from those recordings: whole scenes, and whole spoken segments inside them, never a line cut in half, in the order of the story. The rules it was cut by

Each principle is a rule in the script, and each is checked on the result.

  • Open strong and fast. The council, the stranger at the gate, Athena setting the son his course; then the film crosses straight to Ogygia (Hermes, Calypso, Odysseus weeping on the shore: Book V, scenes 1-3), which in the poem happens while Telemachus sails. Odysseus is on screen at 4.5 min instead of 38.9. The Telemachy keeps its essentials: the loom, Nestor, Athena's eagle, the first recognition, Helen, the horse, Proteus, the ambush. That is the one move out of the id order; everything else plays as the film does.
  • A set piece or a turn every three to four minutes. Longest wait 9.5 min in the full film, 3.4 min in the cut (the rule is 4); within the rule: yes. A set piece is a scene whose spectacle score reaches 0.5 (creatures, set pieces, environments, divine effects, the film's performing objects and short-film cues, and narrated action); a turn is a recognition, the bow, the summit.
  • No more than two talk-only scenes in a row. The full film has a run of 7; the cut's longest is 2: yes.
  • Keep every recognition and the summit. 13 recognitions, all kept: yes; the bow: yes; the bed: yes.
  • The tales are the action spine. Books IX-XII: 38.4 → 26.2 min, the most kept of any stretch (68%).
  • The homecoming accelerates. Books XIII-XX keep 46%, the bow and the hall (XXI-XXII) 78%.
  • A short end. After the bed: a bridge, the orchard, the peace. Book XXIV runs 1.5 min (was 7.6).
  • Runtime in 75-90 min: yes · in story order: yes

What the regulars lose, and why it is worth it

The cut drops 50 scenes (55.7 min) and trims the rest by whole lines. What goes:

  • The narrator's scaffolding. Every spoken scene header ("Scene three. The Stranger at the Threshold.") and every speaker cue ("Zeus") — 24.3 minutes of the full film. They were written for listeners without a picture; the film has a title card and a face.
  • The speeches about speeches. Mentor's rebuke, the eagles explained, Nestor's second war story, Orestes as the example, the stores. The Telemachy keeps the son's growing up and loses its politics.
  • The catalogues. The heroines of the past, the judges and punishments, the gifts at Sparta, Theoclymenus. The poem's audience knew those names; ours meets them once and never again.
  • Book XV nearly whole, and most of the second half's hall-talk. The farewell at Sparta, Eumaeus's own story, Irus's challenge, Melantho, the restless night. The disguise plays out in fewer, sharper humiliations: the stool, the fight, the scar.
  • The long end. The suitors' ghosts, Agamemnon's praise of Penelope, Laertes restored, the last clash: after the bed the film is going home, and the regulars can feel it.

Longest dropped scenes: The Eagles Tear the Air (2:01), Mentor Rebukes Ithaca (1:58), Circe Maps the Dangers (1:54), Calypso Receives the Order (1:46), Melantho and Eurymachus Attack (1:43), The Goddess Departs; the Prince Speaks (1:39).

Set pieces lost (1): The Chariot Road to Sparta. Each is carried by a bridge or is a smaller spectacle next to a kept one.

Why it is worth it. The film's argument is recognition: a man nobody knows, known in turn by a son, a dog, a nurse, a wife, a father. Every one of those is still here, and so is every monster. What goes is the poem's patience — the courtesy between men who already know how the story ends. The regulars get the cave at minute 28 instead of 64, the bow at 74 instead of 144, and they never wait more than 4 minutes for something to happen.

What this does not decide: whether the picture can be cut the same way. The film's picture is timed to the whole recordings; a trimmed scene needs its camera re-timed to the kept segments (the at offsets in cut.json), and a bridge needs a picture, which is new work (a map or a montage of existing plates).

How it was scored

A scene's score is 3.0 × story necessity + 2.0 × spectacle + 1.5 × the film's own priorities + 0.8 × production, times a book weight (the tales and the hall up, the long middle down), minus 1.2 × talk that nothing on screen relieves. Necessity (0-3) is authored, with a reason, for all 152 scenes; everything else is read from the halfworld data: atlas asset types, scenes/_artifacts.mjs, scenes/_direction.mjs, harness/build-sundance.mjs, and the LEGO line's keyframes, kits and film sets (locations.json). A scene is kept if it is a recognition, a turn, necessity 3, or scores 1.5 or more. Inside a kept scene each voice segment is valued by its kind and speech act (from viewer/performance-turns.json), with bonuses for the scene's key beat, a short-film cue, the exit state and the establishing action; the lowest value per second goes first, never below a floor (event 62%, tales 55%, talk 30%, other 40% of the scene's voice) and never the key beat, the short-film cue or a short exit line.
