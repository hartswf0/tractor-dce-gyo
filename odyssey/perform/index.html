The Performance Engine

The acted sheets moved every figure in four drawings out of five, and read as restless puppets. The engine measures the other thing: whether a figure's state changes for a reason. Words become intents, intents become the body's keys on the film's own minifig rigs, and every key has a cause you can ask for. A stillness is a held moment with a reason. Three temperatures and an Ashby homeostat regulate the whole without ever animating anything themselves.

1. The score

Lanes from meaning to mechanics: voice, stimulus, intent, action, contact above the body (root, weight, torso, head, gaze, arms, hands, legs, face, prop) and the world (camera, light, set, effects). Every event names its causes and their latency.

2. The intent compiler

An utterance (speaker, addressee, phrases, stresses, speech act, affect, goal) becomes an intent with parameters; the intent becomes keys: before the voice, on the stress, after, the transition. Patch the intent and the body is derived again.

3. Coupling

Props with grips and ownership (the spear handed over), constraints (a man bound to a mast), one phase clock for rowers, a chain from oars to hull to rope to a straining torso, combat as relations where each action causes the next.

4. Metrics

Each figure, each drawing: action, reaction, alive hold or dead. Coverage, unmotivated freeze and action, reaction latency, gestures per phrase, channel diversity, contact integrity, pose legality from the LDraw parts.

5. Three temperatures

Motion heat (joint and root velocities, conducted through what couples bodies), causal heat (the entropy of each actor's reachable actions), media temperature (McLuhan: how much the shot defines). Contrast and viability are built on them.

6. The homeostat

Four units watch four essential variables. When one leaves its band, its uniselector steps the compiler's parameters to a new position; the first configuration with all four in band is kept, every attempt logged. The coupled version runs the Blinding.

What this is, and is not

Motion heat is an activity variable with the form of a temperature: velocities squared, integrated with a time constant, passed along couplings by a stated table of materials. It is not a simulation of physical heat. Causal heat is the entropy of a declared, hand-written model of each actor's options; its numbers are only as good as the declarations shown above. McLuhan's hot and cool are about how much a medium specifies and how much the audience completes; the media temperature here measures the cut (what is shown, how large, whether cause and effect share a shot), not thermodynamics.
Polyphemus, the Laestrygonians and Scylla are rigs (film-readymades/creatures.js) the engine performs from the same score: the giant's jaw on his own words, his eye shut by the needle's crossing and put out by the thrust, the roar, the heavy walk, the grope; a thrown rock is a parabola with its causes. The take loads a sheet's creatures in place of the staged prop. His arousal (the POLYPHEMUS needle) remains a heat source beside the rig's own motion. Wolves, lions and pigs (Circe) have no rigs yet, and TRANSFORM is not played. The sea is an actor in the storm scenes: a wave field whose level the scene's steps raise, driving the hulls and carrying the swimmers. The camera is the take's own shot plan: the engine records requests for the cut (follow the hot spot, elide an effect) on the camera lane but does not rewrite the shots. The layout (the blocking) is kept: the engine retimes a layout move as a whole when a cause asks for it, and cancels one where a constraint forbids it (the mast).
