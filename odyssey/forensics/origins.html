Forensic report · chapter two

Origins and assembly: the halfworld, the forage, the agents

The LEGO Odyssey did not start in this repository. Its scenes, its spoken lines, its voices and its music came from an earlier project, the halfworld, where an adaptation prompt and fanned-out agents drew the whole poem as halftone programs. This chapter follows that history from its first commit, across the bridge into LEGO, to the way this session assembled the films. Each claim cites a commit (hw marks the halfworld's) or a file; where the record is silent, the chapter says so.

The halfworld

The card and the atlas

The adaptation prompt, PROMPT-HALFWORLD.md, is meant to be pasted "into Claude with any book". It names the unit of pacing ("Books/cantos/chapters are the unit of pacing — build a few, verify, then fan out"), then six phases: the atlas (each scene as causal beats, its assets, and the state handed to the next scene), the engine (the dot law, keying, ink cut-outs, restaging), the contracts, agentic generation, the front-ends and the music. The atlas itself was parsed from a markdown file outside the repository (harness/parse-atlas.mjs reads odyssey_complete_two_view_scene_atlas.md from a local Downloads folder); that file is not in git.

Drawings that are programs

An asset is an ES module of about 200 to 400 lines whose draw() paints flat tone, never dots (engine/asset-contract.mjs). The engine's one post-pass, dotifyField, prints the composed frame on a lattice: lightness L, darkness d = 1 - L, nothing below d = 0.10, otherwise a disc of radius d0.9 · Δ · 0.62 (README.md:46). The README places this in the lineage of Ken Knowlton's BEFLIX (Bell Labs, 1963). There are 432 asset modules and 152 scene programs in the tree today. The later engine files are also agent work: engine/guise.mjs and engine/blocking.mjs (hw e0acb19), engine/liveness.mjs (hw edd1cec: breath on a 4.1 s period, weight drift 9.3 s, "pure functions of (seed,t)"), engine/speech.mjs (hw d5791e7).

The bridge into LEGO

Three steps, three hands. On 2026-09-15 Watson Hartsoe committed a corpus of real LDraw models for the Odyssey and a gap audit (56aa48b0 to e9352284, 11 commits, no trailers). On 2026-09-16 the earlier Claude session (01MNSfMe, 61 commits from 348a3694 to d64f440b) put scenes on those sets (a173797e). On 2026-09-23 this session linked the halfworld's cards to LDraw builds there (hw cd01bed) and, a minute later, foraged all 582 cards here (52030958).

The forage turns words into bricks by rule. tools/forage/table.js gives each type a way of being got: "character: a minifigure from real parts, dressed by role (god, hero, beggar, shade, suitor …)"; "location: a sub-build foraged from a real set (the King's Castle for a hall, the Barracuda for a harbour, the Lincoln Memorial for Olympus, Skull Island for a cave)"; "prop: the readymade part the thing is". A seeded random hand varies a card the same way on every run. tools/forage/ldraw.js then holds the build to account: every part resolved in the library, "every stud of every piece tested against the undersides above it", every piece traced to the plate through stud joints or contact, the rest named as floating. The card files say who did it: 0 Author: word to world, tools/odyssey-forage.js, with donors credited in their FILE headers. There are 871 card files now: the 863 the report counted and the 8 cards of the making-of studio. The staged keyframes, the director's modules and the props were written the same way, by the agent, as files the engine reads.
