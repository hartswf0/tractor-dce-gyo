# The Long Way Home

*Nolan: vast, grave, sincere; time and memory* · 120.00 s · 26 shots

## The idea

Christopher Nolan's register, taken seriously in bricks. The subject is time: ten years at war, ten at sea, and twenty in the house where a woman unpicks her weaving and a dog waits on a dung heap. The trailer opens on the drums of Trial of the Bow, four single strokes that each die to silence, and cuts only on them: the wooden horse alone on the shore, a man clinging to a fig tree over black water, two cards. Then one long build under the voices of the story (Athena, Odysseus, the Cyclops, Tiresias, the nurse), each line spoken over one held image. The build stops dead on the bow, there is black, the only loud passage in the trailer carries the beggar onto the threshold, and it ends on the embrace in the olive-tree room with Zeus's decree: “Odysseus goes home.” The one exception to “no narrator” is “After twenty years”, three words of the epic narrator over Argos.

## Rules this cut follows

1. One idea per shot, and most shots hold five seconds or more; only the three shots inside the drop are shorter.
2. Scale through a tiny figure against vast ground or sky (the horse on the empty shore, a man on a fig tree over the dark, a giant against the sky).
3. Light is hard and low or it is night: dusk backlight, moonlight, one warm practical indoors; no flat daylight.
4. At most one voice line per shot, spoken over a still or slowly moving frame; no line crosses a cut.
5. The words are the characters' own recorded lines, never a synopsis; no narrator explains the plot.
6. Title cards separate the movements and each card lands on a drum.
7. The music is drums and one long build; it stops dead before the drop, and the drop is the only loud passage.
8. Silence before the drop, and silence after it: the ending is room tone, one line and one drum.
9. Restraint: no monster is shown whole, no spectacle is lingered on; the sea, the dead and the dog carry it.

## Why each shot

1. Silence first: the trailer starts before the music does; the first drum is the first cut.
2. The war is over: one enormous object on an empty shore (the Nolan graves-in-the-dunes frame, in bricks).
3. Time is the subject; the card lands on the second drum.
4. One man, very small, in a great dark: scale through a tiny figure against vast ground.
5. The second decade; the fourth drum.
6. Someone remembers him: the gods, far off and bright, and the first spoken line.
7. Held against his will: the raft on a black sea, a man steering by the stars.
8. The thing he longs for is in the frame and he is asleep: memory and loss in one image.
9. Sincerity: the longing said plainly, over a man building his own way home.
10. The curse that makes the way long: a giant against the sky, the camera rising with his prayer.
11. The curse answered, one image per clause.
12. All his comrades lost: seen from below, the way he saw it.
13. The prophecy spoken in the land of the dead: a quiet line, a still image.
14. Endurance, and memory as consolation: the line the whole cut turns on.
15. The third movement is named.
16. He comes home asleep: no line, the picture carries it.
17. Twenty years measured by a dog: the most sincere image in the poem.
18. The one who waited: her work is unmaking time.
19. Recognition, in the words of the one who recognises him.
20. The build peaks on the bow; the score is at its loudest.
21. Silence before the drop.
22. The drop: the beggar becomes the king.
23. The feat, one image.
24. The reckoning, one image; the slam ends with it.
25. The end of the long way, said as the gods decreed it at the start.
26. Title on one drum, then nothing.
