# Trailer reference: what we are measuring ourselves against

Three benchmarks, studied before any cut was written: the studio trailer for Nolan's *The Odyssey* (23 frames in
`films/ref/odyssey-trailer/`, named by their time in seconds), *The LEGO Movie*, and the first brick trailer we made
(`films/odyssey-trailer.mp4`, built in the cinerium). Last, how the voice and the music were measured.

---

### The rules taken from it

1. **One idea per shot.** Every frame above can be named in four words.
2. **Scale through tiny figures against vast ground.** 16.5, 51, 57, 68.5 and 75.5 put people at a fraction of the frame's height.
3. **IMAX-wide horizons,** a quarter to a third up the frame, and whole halves left to sky, haze or black (40, 44, 68.5).
4. **Hard low sun or night with one warm source.** Daylight is overcast haze; the drama is dusk backlight (51, 57) or blue night
   with a single torch, brazier or candle (47, 71, 82, 102.5). The palette is two colours: blue and fire.
5. **Silhouettes and frames within frames** (19.5, 31, 68.5): we look through things at people.
6. **Restraint about monsters.** The Cyclops is never seen; fear is in faces (61, 71). The storm is spray and a face (88-97).
7. **Movement into depth.** Men walk away from us into mist or dark (31, 66); the journey is a direction.
8. **Rhythm.** The frames are 2.5-6 s apart through the first minute (long holds), close to 2-3 s through the storm, and the
   trailer ends on one close, quiet frame of touch. From the brief and the form: title cards between movements, silence and a
   single drum, silence before the drop.
9. **Sincerity.** No irony anywhere; the last image is a hand on a face.

## 2. The LEGO Movie: what it does

1. **Pace.** Two-second shots and stacked jokes, but every sequence is a set-up and a pay-off; the pace comes from cutting on the
   pay-off, not from shortening everything.
2. **Wit comes from character.** Batman's vanity, Benny's “spaceship!”, Emmet's cheerfulness: the jokes are people being who they
   are, and the funniest lines are said straight.
3. **Sincerity under the jokes.** The film turns out to be about a father and a son; the jokes earn the turn because they were never
   cruel. A trailer in this register needs one place where the music drops away and it means it.
4. **Every brick real.** Water, fire, smoke and explosions are made of parts (studs, flame elements, transparent plates); nothing is
   drawn that could not be built, and the minifigure's limits (no knees, clip hands, swappable heads) are the comedy.
5. **The camera is a stop-motion photographer's.** Moves step on twos, lenses are macro-like with shallow focus that says “small”,
   there is weight and a little wobble to the camera; it never floats.
6. **Sound designed to the brick.** Plastic clicks and clacks under the footsteps and the builds, hand-made effects where a film
   would use a roar; the title builds itself from bricks.
7. **A huge final montage** on the music's return, and **a tag after the title.**

## 3. The first brick trailer, judged honestly

`films/odyssey-trailer.mp4` (116 s, 18 shots of which 5 are title cards; `world/scenes-trailer.js`, cut shot for shot against the
studio frames on borrowed cinerium sets). Twelve frames, taken at 4.0, 13.5, 23.0, 32.5, 42.0, 51.5, 61.0, 70.5, 80.0, 89.5, 99.0
and 108.5 s:

| t | frame | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 4.0 | “THE ODYSSEY / A TRAILER IN BRICKS”, small cyan sans on black | a default caption, not designed type |
| 13.5 | the dune graves: black helmets huge in the foreground, the dunes a smooth brown plane | no horizon, no sky, no scale; the graves read as clutter |
| 23.0 | the beachhead: yellow helmets in close-up; the subtitle “Odysseus begins the account of the disasters that followed Troy.” | a synopsis read by a narrator, not a line |
| 32.5 | the pine ridge: trees as brown bars, figures at medium in flat light | the composition copied, the light and mist not |
| 42.0 | the galleys at dusk against a gradient | the best frame; but the ships are a box and a tower and read as buildings |
| 51.5 | the tow: a red cape and a row of figures on grey sand beside a stack of bricks | no gold backlight, no crowd, no scale |
| 61.0 | the cavern: flat walls and the cinerium's own ceiling trusses; Polyphemus a black box | reads as a warehouse; the caption says what we cannot see |
| 70.5 | the ashen shore: helmets in a cluster at dusk | acceptable light, no ground |
| 80.0 | “THIS SUMMER” | a card from another kind of trailer |
| 89.5 | the deck: faces in close-up with painted eyes | no storm, no weather |
| 99.0 | the chamber: heads in extreme close-up under a long caption in tiny type | the one intimate line, illegible |
| 108.5 | “THE ODYSSEY” | the title without a hit |

What it lacks:

1. **It copied the reference's layout instead of its principles.** Each studio frame was rebuilt as a literal patch on a borrowed
   set; so the dunes, the ridge, the citadel are primitives (boxes, walls, towers), and none of them is one of our real, checked kits.
2. **No scale.** Close-ups dominate; there is no tiny figure against vast ground anywhere.
3. **Flat light.** Almost nothing is backlit or low-sun; the night scenes are lit, not dark.
4. **Words are synopsis.** The narrator reads the drive-script beats (“The Cyclops seizes two men…”). And the captions do not match
   the audio: “Hear me now, Polyphemus” is laid over OD-B09-S11 at 9.22 s, where the recording says “Cyclops! It was no weak man's
   friends…”; Penelope's “Do not be angry with me…” is captioned over OD-B23-S04 at 37.88 s, where the recording is the summary
   “Fear of deception caused her delay”; Zeus's line is credited to the narrator.
5. **Generic cards and no rhythm.** Cyan sans, “THIS SUMMER”, five cards spaced evenly; no movements, no drum, no silence, no drop,
   no escalation. Five score files are used once each rather than one piece of music being cut to.
6. **It is not the film.** None of the 31 scenes with gate-checked keyframes, none of the kits: the trailer sells sets the film does
   not have.

The new cuts answer each point: the pictures are the film's own gate-checked keyframes and kits; every line is a recorded line
verified against its audio; every cut that should land on the music does.

## 4. How the voice was verified

The recorded voice is `drive/voice/<scene>.m4a`, segmented by `drive/voice-manifest.json` (`gi` is the index into that scene's
segments in `drive/drive-script.json`). For every candidate segment (67 of them, `lines.json`), the audio was decoded free with
pocketsphinx (en-us) and compared with the two texts it could have been recorded from: the drive-script's text and, for a DIALOGUE
turn, `viewer/spoken-lines.json`'s line. The better match was then force-aligned to the audio, giving every word a start and end;
the trailer clips are cut on those word times (0.12 s before the first word, 0.18 s after the last, never into a neighbouring word).

Findings the render pipeline needs:

- **Most dialogue is recorded from spoken-lines.json, but not all.** Nine dialogue segments speak the drive-script's summary
  sentence instead of the character's line (OD-B21-S04 gi 3, OD-B21-S07 gi 5 and 8, OD-B22-S01 gi 6, OD-B23-S03 gi 5, OD-B23-S04
  gi 2 and 7, OD-B23-S05 gi 6, OD-B24-S03 gi 5 and 7). So “You dogs. You never thought I would come home from Troy…” was never
  recorded; the audio says “Names himself and accuses them of consuming his house and violating his servants.” Penelope's bed test
  is recorded only as “Move the marriage bed outside the chamber for the stranger.”
- **Three of those appear to start with a spoken direction** (the free decode hears “never announce yourself … never read flatly”
  at the head of OD-B21-S07 gi 5, OD-B22-S01 gi 6 and OD-B23-S05 gi 6).
- **“Tell me, O Muse, of the man of many devices” is not in the recording at all**; no book opens with it.
- **The narrator is slow**: about one word a second with long pauses, so a narration sentence is 6-11 s; the trailers use
  sub-clips of lines rather than whole segments.

## 5. How the music was measured

Every track in `audio/` was measured with ffmpeg's ebur128 filter (momentary 400 ms and short-term 3 s loudness, every 0.1 s) to map
its builds, drops and silences; hit times were then refined from a 10 ms RMS trace of the waveform. The ones the cuts use:

| track | what is where (track seconds) |
|---|---|
| Trial of the Bow (3:32) | four single drums, each decaying to about −48 LUFS, peaking at 1.20, 6.00, 11.00, 16.00; a sharp hit at 26.05; loud drive 60-110; silence 162-178.6; slam at 179.50 |
| The King of Ithaca (2:15) | starts near silence (−58), one long build to −8 at 113-116, the peak ends at 116.00, gone by 124 |
| Hearthside Soul (3:24) | quiet 66-102; silence 175-184.65; a 7 s slam from 184.70 at −8 LUFS that stops by itself at 191.5 |
| Circe's Loom and the Bagged Gales (2:27) | driving 4-73, phrase hits 21.46 and 42.65; drops at 73.05 to a soft pulse (−19 to −26) until the return at 100.85 |
| Boulder-Drop Bass (3:24) | quiet to 24, entry 24.2, big hit 56.5, near-silence 87.0-94.8, build 95-120, dip 122.9-126.3, slam 126.35 |
| Underworld Descent (2:41) | swell to 3.5, silence 8.5-12.5, swell to 16.0, silence 21-24.5, hit 25.35, loud to 31 |

The in-points in each cut are chosen so these land on cuts; `trailers.py` checks that every declared hit is within 0.06 s of a cut.
