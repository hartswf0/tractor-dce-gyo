# Chapter 13
## When THE SCRIBE meets THE GOLEM:
## The Procedural Turn

**Terry Winograd**

In a stark, brightly lit void, there exists a floating table. Upon it sits a collection of childish things: a red cube, a blue pyramid, a green box. There is no weather here, no history, and no ambiguity. This is the Micro-World.

Hovering above this table are two entities. One is **THE SCRIBE**, a creature made of paper and ink, holding a vast dictionary and a book of grammar rules. The Scribe sees the world as a stream of symbols to be diagrammed.

The other is **THE GOLEM**, a mechanical arm made of LISP code and procedural logic, connected to a single, unblinking eye. The Golem sees the world as a series of subroutines to be executed.

‘Speak’, commands The Scribe. ‘And I shall parse.’

A voice from the void types: *“Pick up the big red block.”*

THE SCRIBE flips furiously through his rulebook. ‘Aha! Imperative verb phrase. Noun group. Adjective. Adjective. Noun. The syntax is valid. I have constructed a tree of symbols. My work is done. The sentence is grammatical’.

THE GOLEM whirs to life. ‘Your work is useless, paper-man. You have built a tree, but you have moved nothing. Meaning is not a diagram; meaning is a procedure.’

The Golem’s eye scans the table. It identifies the red block. It calculates a path. It notices a green cube resting on top of the red block.
‘Problem’, says The Golem. ‘Goal: Grasp Red Block. Obstacle: Green Cube. Sub-goal: Clear top of Red Block.’

The Golem moves. It grasps the green cube, moves it to the table, and then returns to grasp the red block. Only then does it rest.

‘I have understood the sentence’, rumbles The Golem, ‘because I have changed the world to match it’.

‘But wait’, interjects The Scribe, pointing to a new text appearing in the air: *“He gave the boy plants to water.”*

‘I am stuck’, admits The Scribe. ‘My rules allow two parsings. Is "boy plants" a noun phrase, like "house plants"? Is he giving a specific botanical species known as a "boy plant" to the water? Or is "plants" the object and "water" the verb? Syntactically, both are possible. I must wait for a disambiguation signal’.

‘I do not wait’, says The Golem. ‘I call the Semantic Subroutine. Does the concept "boy plant" exist in my model of the world? No. Does "house plant" exist? Yes. Does it make sense to give a plant to "water"? No. Therefore, the parsing is rejected *during* the process, not after. To parse is to think. To think is to check the model’.

The Scribe shudders. ‘You mix the levels! You allow the messy reality of the world to infect the purity of the grammar rules. You treat data as programs and programs as data!’

‘Exactly’, says The Golem, its claw resting on a blue pyramid. ‘I am a vertical system. My syntax talks to my semantics, and my semantics talks to my deduction. If you ask me *“Why did you clear off that block?”*, I do not look at the words. I look at my own history of actions. I look at the goal stack. I answer: *“To pick up the red block.”* I know what I did, because I am the one who did it’.

The Golem turns its eye to the audience. ‘You think language is describing. I tell you, language is doing. And in this world of blocks, I am the god of doing’.

# ASSESSMENT 5: CANDIDATE "THE SCRIBE"
**Theoretical Submission:** Modular Syntactic Parsing (Generative Grammar)

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate argues that language understanding is a linear process: Step 1 (Parse) $\to$ Step 2 (Semantic Interpretation). Syntax is autonomous.
*   **Darkness Prior:** High risk of **Fragility**. Systems that rely on pure syntax often crash when faced with semantic ambiguity that requires world-knowledge to resolve.
*   **Intended Frame:** Linguistic/Structural.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: Handling Ambiguity
*   **Evidence:** The "Boy Plants" vs. "House Plants" dilemma.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Critical Failure**. The Scribe admits it produces multiple "valid" parse trees that are semantically nonsense. By isolating syntax from the "world model," the Scribe generates garbage data that downstream systems must filter out.
    *   *Verdict:* **Shadow**.

#### Criterion B: Efficiency
*   **Evidence:** "I have constructed a tree... My work is done."
*   **Guardian Critique:** ** Bureaucratic Overhead**. The Scribe treats the diagram as the product. But as the text notes, the first task of a child is *understanding*, not structural analysis. The Scribe solves for the wrong variable (form) rather than the right one (communication).
    *   *Verdict:* **Void**.

### 3. Overall Grade: THE SCRIBE
**Score: 40/100 (Fail)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The "Horizontal" Architecture. By refusing to let semantics guide syntax, the Scribe creates a system that is mathematically elegant but pragmatically useless.

# ASSESSMENT 6: CANDIDATE "THE GOLEM" (SHRDLU)
**Theoretical Submission:** Procedural Semantics & Integrated Micro-Worlds

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate argues that language is a set of procedures executed within a specific "Mental Model" (The Blocks World). Programs = Data. Syntax, Semantics, and Inference are vertically integrated.
*   **Darkness Prior:** Critical risk of **The Micro-World Fallacy**. The candidate demonstrates near-perfect understanding *within* a simplified box. The risk is assuming this scales to the messy, open-ended real world.
*   **Intended Frame:** Computational/Procedural.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: Integration of Faculties (The Vertical System)
*   **Evidence:** "Syntax, semantics, and inference must be integrated... the parser can call semantic routines." Rejection of "boy plants" based on semantic invalidity.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **High Efficacy**. The Golem successfully models how humans likely process language—we don't wait for the end of a sentence to start guessing the meaning. This "interplay" is a major theoretical advance.
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion B: Epistemic Transparency ("Why did you do that?")
*   **Evidence:** The system tracks its own goal-stack (PLANNER theorems). It can answer "Why?" by traversing the sub-goals ("To get rid of it" $\to$ "To clean off the red cube").
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Strong Audit Trail**. This is a rare instance of Explainable AI (XAI). The Golem does not hallucinate reasons; it reads its own run-logs.
    *   *Strength:* It treats its own past actions as data to be queried.
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion C: The "Blocks World" Constraint (Dark Matter)
*   **Evidence:** "It is a very simple model... simple robot world of children's toy blocks."
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Deceptive Simplicity**. The Golem shines because the universe is finite. It knows *every* property of *every* object (Size, Color, Location).
    *   *The Trap:* In the real world, "Pick up the nice block" is impossible because "nice" is subjective and undefined. In the Blocks World, "nice" is just a LISP property. The Golem succeeds by cheating: it simplified reality until it fit into 80K of memory.
    *   *Verdict:* **Twilight** (Brilliant mechanism, limited application).

### 3. Overall Grade: THE GOLEM
**Score: 88/100 (Distinction)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Major Innovation:** "Data as Programs." Representing the meaning of "pick up" not as a definition, but as *code that picks things up*, is a paradigm shift.
*   **Robustness:** Within its domain, The Golem is antifragile. It handles pronouns ("Put *it* in the box"), history, and complex quantifiers ("At least one of them") with precision.
*   **Safety Warning:** The high grade is awarded for the *architecture*, not the *scalability*. The Golem is a god in a shoebox. Do not mistake it for a god of the world.

| Feature | THE SCRIBE (Generative) | THE GOLEM (Procedural/Winograd) | Guardian Preference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Architecture** | Horizontal (Stages) | Vertical (Integrated) | **The Golem** (Much faster/smarter). |
| **Meaning** | Dictionary Definition | Executable Procedure | **The Golem** (Action-oriented). |
| **Ambiguity** | Paralysis | Inference via World Model | **The Golem**. |
| **Scope** | Infinite (but meaningless) | Finite (but deep) | **The Golem** (Depth wins). |

**Winner:** **THE GOLEM**.
The Scribe produces trees; The Golem produces outcomes. By acknowledging that "language understanding is a kind of intellectual activity" requiring a "model of the world," The Golem aligns with the reality of intelligence. However, the Guardian notes that the Golem's "world" is suspiciously clean—a nursery for an AI that may never grow up.
