Instead, he narrates **the sequential acts of its fabrication** (*Nacheinander* / *das Werden*), anchoring every scene in an iterative imperfect or aorist verb of making:

- **`Ποίει δὲ πρώτιστα`** (*Il.* 18.478 — *"First of all he was forging..."*)
- **`Ἐν μὲν γαῖαν ἔτευξ'` (**Il.* 18.483 — *"Therein he wrought Earth..."*)

- **`Ἐν δ' ἐτίθει...`** (*Il.* 18.541, 550, 561, 607 — *"And therein he was placing..."*)

- **Polytonic Greek (Monro & Allen OCT):**
  > Ἐν δ' ἐτίθει σταφυλῇσι μέγα βρίθουσαν ἀλωὴν  
  > καλὴν χρυσείην· μέλανες δ' ἀνὰ βότρυες ἦσαν,  
  > ἑστήκει δὲ κάμαξι διαμπερὲς ἀργυρέῃσιν.  
  > ἀμφὶ δὲ κυανέην κάπετον, περὶ δ' ἕρκος ἔλασσε  
  > κασσιτέρου· μία δ' οἴη ἀταρπιτὸς ἦεν ἐπ' αὐτήν...  
  > τοῖσιν δ' ἐν μέσσοισι πάϊς φόρμιγγι λιγείῃ  
  > ἱμερόεν κιθάριζε, λίνον δ' ὑπὸ καλὸν ἄειδε  
  > λεπταλέῃ φωνῇ· τοὶ δὲ ῥήσσοντες ἁμαρτῇ  
  > μολπῇ τ' ἰυγμῷ τε ποσὶ σκαίροντες ἕποντο.
- **Spoken Wordtext:**  
  *And therein he set a vineyard heavily laden with clusters, fair and wrought of gold, yet black were the grapes upon it, and the vines stood propped throughout on poles of silver. Around it he drew a trench of dark blue enamel, and a fence of tin; and one single pathway led to it, whereby the vintagers went whenever they gathered the vintage. And in their midst a boy made lovely music upon a clear-toned lyre, and sang the delicate Linus-song with his tender voice, while the others beat the earth in time with song and shouting and leaping feet.*
- **`THEREFORE →` Causal Ripple:** Because the grain harvest of late summer leads into the autumn vintage, **therefore** Hephaestus brings all rare alloys together in one poly-metallic enclosure: golden vines, black grape-clusters, silver poles (`κάμαξι ἀργυρέῃσιν`), a trench of dark-blue `κύανος` enamel, and a fence of tin (`κασσιτέρου`).
- **`Mask Filter`:** Even in the sweetest moment of vintage joy, the boy with the clear-toned phorminx sings the `λίνος` song (`λίνον δ' ὑπὸ καλὸν ἄειδε λεπταλέῃ φωνῇ`)—a traditional lament for a youth who dies in his prime, quietly echoing Achilles's own fate.
- **`Scholia Recentiora`:** Nagy (1990) and Edwards (1991: 224–226) note that the `λίνος` song is an internal *mise en abyme* of lyric lament embedded inside heroic hexameter: art mourns mortality at the very height of ripeness.

### §IX. The Herd of Gold & Tin and the Two Terrible Lions (*Iliad* 18.573–586)
- **Operative Formula:** `Ἐν δ' ἀγέλην ποίησε βοῶν ὀρθοκραιράων` (*"And therein he made a herd of straight-horned cattle..."*)
- **Tectonic Locus:** Outer Ring 3 North (`M7` · `[-14, 54, -644]`) · **Bricks `5,116 → 5,695` (`+579 bricks`)** · **`184.58s – 205.51s`**
- **Polytonic Greek (Monro & Allen OCT):**
  > Ἐν δ' ἀγέλην ποίησε βοῶν ὀρθοκραιράων·  
  > αἱ δὲ βόες χρυσοῖο τετεύχατο κασσιτέρου τε,  
  > μυκηθμῷ δ' ἀπὸ κόπρου ἐπεσσεύοντο νομόνδε  
  > παρὰ ποταμὸν κελάδοντα, παρὰ ῥοδανὸν δονακῆα.  
  > χρύσειοι δὲ νομῆες ἅμ' ἐστιχόωντο βόεσσι  
  > τέσσαρες, ἐννέα δέ σφι κύνες πόδας ἀργοὶ ἕποντο.  
  > σμερδαλέω δὲ λέοντε δύ' ἐν πρώτῃσι βόεσσι  
  > ταῦρον ἐρύγμηλον ἐχέτην· ὁ δὲ μακρὰ μεμυκὼς  
  > ἕλκετο· τὸν δὲ κύνες μετεκίαθον ἠδ' αἰζηοί.  
  > τὼ μὲν ἀναρρήξαντε βοὸς μεγάλοιο βοείην  
  > ἔγκατα καὶ μέλαν αἷμα λαφύσσετον...
- **Spoken Wordtext:**  
  *And therein he made a herd of straight-horned cattle: the kine were wrought of gold and of tin, and with lowing they rushed from the farmyard to pasture beside a sounding river, beside the swaying reed-bed. Four golden herdsmen walked beside the cattle, and nine swift-footed hounds followed them. But two terrible lions among the foremost kine held fast a loud-bellowing bull; he was dragged away bellowing mightily, and the hounds and young men rushed after him. Yet the two lions, having rent the hide of the great bull, gulped down the dark blood and the entrails, while the herdsmen in vain set on the swift hounds, who shrank back from the jaws of the lions and stood close by and barked.*
- **`THEREFORE →` Causal Ripple:** Because human agriculture pushes outward into the untamed reed-beds by the sounding river (`παρὰ ποταμὸν κελάδοντα`), **therefore** the pastoral order collides with the raw predator-law of nature: two terrible lions (`σμερδαλέω δὲ λέοντε`) seize the bellowing bull (`ταῦρον ἐρύγμηλον`).
- **`Mask Filter`:** Here the `Mask Filter` of the *Iliad*'s battlefield lion-similes breaks directly onto the surface of the Shield: even golden herdsmen (`χρύσειοι νομῆες`) and nine swift hounds are paralyzed before apex violence, standing close and barking but shrinking from the bite (`ἀλλὰ μάλ' ἐγγὺς ἱστάμενοι ὑλάοντο ἔκ τ' ἀλέοντο`).
- **`Scholia Recentiora`:** Lonsdale (1990, *Creatures of Speech*) and Taplin (1980: 10) observe that this scene imports the epic simile world directly into the ekphrasis, showing that pastoral peace cannot abolish violent predation.

### §X. The Sheep-Glen & The Cretan Choros of Daedalus (*Iliad* 18.587–606)
- **Operative Formula:** `Ἐν δὲ χορὸν ποίκιλλε περικλυτὸς Ἀμφιγυήεις` (*"And therein the famed Lame God intricately wrought a dancing-floor..."*)
- **Tectonic Locus:** Outer Ring 3 South-East (`M8` · `[538, 62, 372]`) · **Bricks `5,695 → 6,212` (`+517 bricks`)** · **`205.51s – 228.59s`**
- **Polytonic Greek (Monro & Allen OCT):**
  > Ἐν δὲ νομὸν ποίησε περικλυτὸς Ἀμφιγυήεις  
  > ἐν καλῇ βήσσῃ μέγαν οἰῶν ἀργεννάων...  
  > Ἐν δὲ χορὸν ποίκιλλε περικλυτὸς Ἀμφιγυήεις,  
  > τῷ ἴκελον οἷόν ποτ' ἐνὶ Κνωσῷ εὐρείῃ  
  > Δαίδαλος ἤσκησεν καλλιπλοκάμῳ Ἀριάδνῃ.  
  > ἔνθα μὲν ἠΐθεοι καὶ παρθένοι ἀλφεσίβοιαι  
  > ὠρχεῦντ' ἀλλήλων ἐπὶ καρπῷ χεῖρας ἔχοντες...  
  > οἱ δ' ὁτὲ μὲν θρέξασκον ἐπισταμένοισι πόδεσσι  
  > ῥεῖα μάλ', ὡς ὅτε τις τροχὸν ἄρμενον ἐν παλάμῃσιν  
  > ἑζόμενος κεραμεὺς πειρήσεται, αἴ κε θέῃσιν·  
  > ἄλλοτε δ' αὖ θρέξασκον ἐπὶ στίχας ἀλλήλοισι.  
  > πολλὸς δ' ἱμερόεντα χορὸν περίσταθ' ὅμιλος  
  > τερπόμενοι· δοιὼ δὲ κυβιστητῆρε κατ' αὐτοὺς  
  > μολπῆς ἐξάρχοντες ἐδίνεον κατὰ μέσσους.
- **Spoken Wordtext:**  
  *And therein the famed Lame God made a large pasture of white sheep in a fair mountain glen, with folds and roofed huts and pens. And therein the famed Lame God intricately wrought a dancing-floor, like unto that which once in broad Knossos Daedalus fashioned for fair-haired Ariadne. There youths and maidens worth many cattle danced, holding one another's hands at the wrist, clad in fine linen and tunics softly glistening with oil. Now they ran round with skilled feet full lightly, as when a seated potter fits his wheel to his palms and tests it to see whether it will run; and anon they ran in lines to meet one another, while two tumblers whirled through the midst of the joyful crowd.*
- **`THEREFORE →` Causal Ripple:** Because mortal life is bounded on every side by strife and the jaws of lions, **therefore** Hephaestus answers terror with the highest synthesis of human and divine craft (`ποίκιλλε`): the circular and linear dance (`χορός`) where youths and maidens lock wrists (`ἐπὶ καρπῷ χεῖρας ἔχοντες`).
- **`Mask Filter`:** Hephaestus compares his own metalwork to two mortal craftsmen—**Daedalus in broad Knossos** (`Δαίδαλος ... ἐνὶ Κνωσῷ`) and a **seated potter testing his wheel** (`κεραμεὺς πειρήσεται`)—creating a triple reflection between blacksmith, architect, potter, and poet.
- **`Scholia Recentiora`:** Frontisi-Ducroux (1975, *Dédale*) and Purves (2010) note that `ποίκιλλε` (used only here in the *Hoplopoiia*) marks the pinnacle of variegated craft: circular motion (`τροχός`, matching the concentric rings of the Shield) alternates with linear rows (`ἐπὶ στίχας`, matching the hexameter verses of the poem).

### §XI. The Uttermost Rim — The Mighty Stream of Oceanus (*Iliad* 18.607–608)
- **Operative Formula:** `Ἐν δ' ἐτίθει ποταμοῖο μέγα σθένος Ὠκεανοῖο` (*"And therein he set the mighty strength of the River Oceanus..."*)
- **Tectonic Locus:** Uttermost Perimeter Rim (`M9` · Ring 4, `r > 580 LDU`) · **Bricks `6,212 → 7,538` (`+1,326 bricks`)** · **`228.59s – 246.39s`**
- **Polytonic Greek (Monro & Allen OCT):**
  > Ἐν δ' ἐτίθει ποταμοῖο μέγα σθένος Ὠκεανοῖο  
  > ἄντυγα πὰρ πυμάτην σάκεος πύκα ποιητοῖο.
- **Spoken Wordtext:**  
  *And therein he set the mighty strength of the River Oceanus, along the uttermost rim of the solidly built shield; sealing the stars, the two cities, the blood-price, the blackening gold of the plough, and the whirling dance within one unbroken horizon of bronze and flowing water.*
- **`THEREFORE →` Causal Ripple:** Because a cosmos of stars, cities, lawsuits, battles, harvests, predators, and dances would spill into chaos without a boundary, **therefore** Hephaestus pours the mighty strength of River Oceanus (`ποταμοῖο μέγα σθένος Ὠκεανοῖο`) around the uttermost rim (`ἄντυγα πὰρ πυμάτην`), locking all **7,538 bricks** into a finite, self-contained world.
- **`Mask Filter`:** Oceanus closes the ring-composition begun at *Il.* 18.489 (`λοετρῶν Ὠκεανοῖο`): when Thetis carries this completed world down from Olympus at dawn (*Il.* 19.1–19), only Achilles among all the Myrmidons dares look directly upon it, and his eyes flash like fire (`ὡς εἰ σέλας`).
- **`Scholia Recentiora`:** Taplin (1980: 11–15) and Scully (2003) conclude that Oceanus transforms the Shield from an open catalogue into a cosmic enclosure (`σάκεος πύκα ποιητοῖο`): Achilles carries into battle the entire totality of human peace, law, agriculture, and joy that his own choice of short-lived glory (`κλέος ἄφθιτον`) forces him to forfeit.
