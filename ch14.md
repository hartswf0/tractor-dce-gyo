# Chapter 14
## When THE NAVIGATOR meets THE CARTOGRAPHER:
## The Map of Grammar

**Terry Winograd**

The forest of Language is dense. It is a tangled thicket of words, recursive vines, and deep roots of meaning. Two explorers stand at the edge, preparing to chart a path through.

One is **THE CARTOGRAPHER** (representing Transformational Grammar/Context-Free Rules). He carries a stack of transparent overlays and a compass fixed on "Deep Structure." He believes the forest is an illusion hiding a perfect, logical grid below.

‘To understand the forest’, The Cartographer declares, ‘we must peel back the chaotic surface. We must generate a "Deep Structure" tree—a pure, context-free skeleton. Then, we apply transformations—twisting and grafting the branches—until we reproduce the messy "Surface Structure" we see before us. We define the forest by rules of generation: *S $\to$ NP + VP*.’

The other explorer is **THE NAVIGATOR** (representing Systemic Grammar/PROGRAMMAR). She carries no fixed maps, but wears a toolbelt filled with procedural gadgets: pointers, registers, and interrupt signals. She views the forest not as a static grid, but as a series of choices to be navigated in real-time.

‘Your maps are beautiful’, says The Navigator, ‘but I am not here to *generate* a forest. I am here to *recognize* a path. I do not care about the "Deep Structure" unless it helps me decide which way to turn right now. My method is not a rule-book; it is a program. It is a set of instructions: "If you see a verb, check its features. If it is Transitive, look for an Object. If you hit the word 'and', stop everything and call the Conjunction Demon."’

The Cartographer scoffs. ‘You are mixing the levels! You pollute the purity of the syntax with semantic checks. You let the meaning of a word decide the grammatical path. This is messy. It is not "perspicuous."’

‘It is efficient’, counters The Navigator. ‘Look at the sentence: *“The giraffe ate the apples and peaches.”* When I hit "and", I do not backtrack to the beginning of time. I call a special routine—a demon—that stitches the "peaches" onto the "apples" right then and there. I treat grammar as a flow-chart of decisions, not a static tree of symbols.’

She points to a complex vine—a Verb Group like *“could have been going to be fixed.”*

‘To you’, she says, ‘this is a nightmare of nested rules. To me, it is a simple loop. I check the first word ("could"). It sets the flag MODAL. I check the next. It sets the flag PAST. I walk down the chain, setting flags on my way. When I am done, I have the meaning: *Modal Past in Future in Passive*. I did not build a tree; I executed a procedure.’

The Navigator steps into the undergrowth. ‘Language is not a crystal to be admired, Cartographer. It is a stream of data to be processed. And the best way to describe a process is not with a rule, but with a program.’

# ASSESSMENT 7: CANDIDATE "THE CARTOGRAPHER"
**Theoretical Submission:** Transformational Generative Grammar (Chomsky/Woods)

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate argues that syntax is best modeled as a set of generative rules that produce "Deep Structure," which is then transformed into surface language.
*   **Darkness Prior:** High risk of **Computational Intractability**. "Unwinding" transformations to parse a sentence is mathematically elegant but exponentially expensive (combinatorial explosion).
*   **Intended Frame:** Mathematical/Formal.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: Parsing Efficiency
*   **Evidence:** The text notes that transformational parsers (Petrick, Zwicky) try to "unwind" rules to find deep structure.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Practical Failure**. The "Inverse Transformational" problem is notoriously difficult. As the text states, this approach has not gone beyond handling small subsets of English efficiently.
    *   *Verdict:* **Shadow**.

#### Criterion B: Handling Ambiguity
*   **Evidence:** Reliance on "Deep Structure" trees.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Rigidity**. By forcing every sentence to fit a specific tree structure *before* considering meaning, the Cartographer generates massive ambiguity that must be pruned later.
    *   *Verdict:* **Void**.

### 3. Overall Grade: THE CARTOGRAPHER
**Score: 50/100 (Passable Theory, Poor Engineering)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The separation of "generation" from "interpretation." A rule that is good for *producing* a sentence is often terrible for *recognizing* one.

# ASSESSMENT 8: CANDIDATE "THE NAVIGATOR" (PROGRAMMAR)
**Theoretical Submission:** Systemic Grammar & Procedural Parsing

### 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest
*   **Core Task:** The candidate models syntax as a program (PROGRAMMAR). Grammar = Code. The parser uses "Systemic Grammar" (Halliday) to focus on *choices* (features) rather than structure trees.
*   **Darkness Prior:** Moderate risk of **Ad-Hoc Engineering**. "Demons" and "Interrupts" are powerful but can lead to "spaghetti code" grammars that are hard to verify or extend.
*   **Intended Frame:** Computational/Pragmatic.

### 2. Forensic Audit & Dark Matter Scan

#### Criterion A: The "Grammar as Program" Paradigm
*   **Evidence:** "A grammar is a set of instructions for parsing." Use of `PARSE`, `ISQ` (is question?), and `BRANCH` logic.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **High Efficacy**. This aligns perfectly with how computers actually work. It allows for "intelligent parsing"—checking semantics *during* the parse (e.g., rejecting "boy plants" early).
    *   *Strength:* Left-branching structures (possessives) are handled by simple loops, avoiding infinite recursion traps common in standard top-down parsers.
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion B: Systemic Features (The Network)
*   **Evidence:** Focus on "Systems" of features (e.g., MOOD: Declarative/Interrogative; TRANSITIVITY: Transitive/Intransitive).
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Semantic Relevance**. Unlike the abstract symbols of the Cartographer ($S \to NP \dots$), Systemic features directly map to *meaning*. Knowing a clause is "Imperative" tells the system how to react (execute a command).
    *   *Verdict:* **Light**.

#### Criterion C: Handling Complexity (The "And" Demon)
*   **Evidence:** "And" is defined as a "Demon" (interrupt) that calls a special program to handle conjunctions dynamically.
*   **Guardian Critique:** **Adversarial Integrity Check**. While effective, this is a "hack" in the noble sense. It bypasses the formal grammar to run a subroutine.
    *   *Risk:* This makes the grammar difficult to analyze mathematically. It trades theoretical purity for engineering performance.
    *   *Verdict:* **Twilight** (Effective but messy).

### 3. Overall Grade: THE NAVIGATOR
**Score: 85/100 (Distinction)**

**Guardian Justification:**
*   **Core Strength:** The "Systemic" approach solves the "So What?" problem of syntax. It parses for *features* that downstream semantic systems can actually use.
*   **Innovation:** The "Augmented Transition Network" (ATN) equivalent. The realization that a grammar is a state-machine with registers (Turing equivalent) allows it to handle the full complexity of English where Context-Free grammars fail.
*   **Safety Warning:** The reliance on "Demons" and specialized LISP functions means the grammar is not portable. It is deeply entangled with the runtime environment.
